Small model lab

If a model starts from nothing, why doesn't every neuron learn the same thing?

A network with 12 neurons per layer learns to separate a two-colour spiral, live in your browser, with the backprop written out by hand. Change how its weights start and watch what breaks.

It never starts from nothing. It starts from random numbers, and that randomness is what lets neurons become different. Start them all equal and every layer stays one neuron copied 12 times, forever. Start them random but at the wrong size and the signal fades away or the neurons die.

Things to try

  1. Scaled random (the default): it learns the spiral in about 40 steps, and every layer keeps 12 of 12 different neurons.
  2. All the same: "Different" drops to 1 of 12 and stays there. Every neuron gets the same blame, so every neuron gets the same update. With tanh it still learns a little, as one neuron per layer.
  3. All zeros: every hidden gradient is exactly 0. Nothing moves except the output bias.
  4. Tiny random: the signal shrinks about 30 times per layer. Set 6 layers and the last one is down around 10-10. The loss sits on the coin-flip line.
  5. Huge random with tanh: most neurons are pinned at ±1, yet at 3 layers it still learns, even faster than scaled. Slide to 6 layers and it usually stalls.
  6. Huge random with ReLU: the first update is so big it pushes every neuron negative. A ReLU that outputs 0 everywhere gets no gradient, so they never come back.
  7. Hit New random draw a few times on scaled: each start takes its own path and draws a slightly different boundary, and they all get there.

What it says

  • Randomness is the tie-breaker. Gradient descent can only make neurons more different than they already are. If they start identical, the maths keeps them identical.
  • The size of the random numbers matters as much as the randomness. Each neuron sums 12 inputs, so the spread grows or shrinks by a fixed factor every layer. Scaled init divides by √(inputs) so that factor is about 1. Too small and the signal fades; too big and ReLUs die.
  • Saturation hurt less than I expected. Huge random with tanh learned faster than scaled at 3 layers. Deep stacks are where it bites: at 6 layers, 2 of 3 random draws stalled.
  • Real language models add LayerNorm, residual connections and smaller per-layer scales on top, which make this much less fragile. This page has none of that, on purpose.

Honest answer: December 18, 1946 wasn't a big headline day. Nothing world-changing is pinned to that exact date, so I'm not going to invent something to make it sound important.

The best thing about the day is a birthday coincidence. Steve Biko, the South African anti-apartheid activist, was born the same day as Spielberg, December 18, 1946. One became Hollywood's biggest director and the other became a symbol of resistance to apartheid, both from the same day.

The world was jumpy right then too. The very next day, December 19, 1946, fighting broke out in Hanoi, and that's usually counted as the start of the First Indochina War, which led to the Vietnam War.

If you mean December 18 as a calendar date in any year, it has more going for it:
- **1865:** The 13th Amendment, which abolished slavery in the US, was officially proclaimed in effect.
- **Qatar's National Day** is December 18.
- **International Migrants Day** (UN) is December 18.
- Keith Richards (1943) and Brad Pitt (1963) also share Spielberg's birthday.

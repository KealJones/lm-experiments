Here is a day-by-day plan for April 12 to 16, 2027, with four nights based at Kyoto Station. I have kept it to about two main activities a day for Maya, with rest built in, and I have marked a few optional swaps. April 12 falls on a Monday, so Days 2 to 5 are weekdays.

**Day 1, Monday April 12: Arrival**
- Land at Kansai International Airport and ask for staff assistance at the arrival gate.
- Take the Haruka express to Kyoto Station. Reserve the wheelchair space in advance.
- Check in and rest. Arriving early in the day leaves room for a nap if the travel tires Maya.
- Late afternoon, take a short, easy stroll through Shosei-en, the small garden near the station.
- Have an early dinner at a restaurant with a step-free entrance, close to the hotel.

**Day 2, Tuesday April 13: Kiyomizu-dera**
- Book the accessible taxi a day or two ahead for a 6:30 a.m. pickup, and ask to be dropped at the entrance nearest the temple gate.
- Spend the morning at Kiyomizu-dera while it is quiet. Ask at the ticket booth about any reduced or free rate for Maya and her companion.
- Ask staff about the current accessible route before you go.
- Have lunch in the Higashiyama area, then return to the hotel by midday.
- Rest through the afternoon. In the evening, keep it light, with a short walk near the hotel or the Shijo area if you feel up to it.

**Day 3, Wednesday April 14: Nara day trip**
- Take the JR Nara line from Kyoto Station, which takes about 45 minutes. The Kintetsu line is a similar option.
- Spend the late morning and early afternoon in Nara Park, where the deer roam, and at Todai-ji, which has a level approach and a ramp into the main hall.
- Return to Kyoto by mid-afternoon and rest at the hotel.
- Keep dinner simple and close to the hotel.

**Day 4, Thursday April 15: Gardens, lunch, and Fushimi Inari**
- In the morning, visit the Kyoto Botanical Garden, which has broad, flat paths and a large greenhouse. Take the subway, as you planned earlier.
- Have the lunch kaiseki. Book ahead, and confirm there is a step-free entrance and a table that fits the wheelchair.
- Rest at the hotel in the early afternoon.
- In the early evening, visit the lower section of Fushimi Inari. Take the JR Nara line from Kyoto Station, which is about five minutes, and use the elevator at the station. Stay near the base and the first gates, and skip the climb. If Maya is tired, drop this visit.

**Day 5, Friday April 16: Departure**
- Leave the hotel with a comfortable buffer, and take the Haruka back to Kansai International Airport.
- Request wheelchair assistance with the airline for the return flight as well.
- If your flight leaves late, you could spend an hour at Nishiki Market on a weekday morning, when it is less crowded, or take a short stroll through the flat Kyoto Imperial Palace Park.

**Optional swaps**
- Nijo Castle and the flat main path at Arashiyama's bamboo grove are good options if you want a different day or have extra time.
- If Maya prefers a shorter day, you could swap Nara for Arashiyama. Arashiyama is much closer to the hotel.

**Budget check**
Meals for two should come to roughly $60 to $100 a day, and taxis, the IC card, and admissions add only a modest amount. Each day should stay comfortably inside your $250 daily budget, with room for a special meal.

**Before you go**
- Reserve the Haruka wheelchair seats, the accessible taxi for Day 2, and the kaiseki lunch for Day 4.
- Confirm the hotel room's doorway width and bathroom access, as discussed.
- Load an ICOCA or Suica card onto your phone, and carry some yen for small shops and temples.
- Bring a cushion and a small repair kit with a tire pump for the cobblestones.
- Check opening hours, admission fees, and accessibility details for each site, since these change and I cannot confirm current figures from here.

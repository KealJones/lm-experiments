User: Let us start with revenue. I sell each mug for 24.50 dollars, and last month I sold 1,360 mugs. What was my monthly revenue?

Assistant: Monthly revenue is the unit price multiplied by the units sold. That is 24.50 × 1,360. Splitting it up: 24 × 1,360 = 32,640, and 0.50 × 1,360 = 680. Added together, that gives 33,320. Your monthly revenue was $33,320.00. In a spreadsheet, if the price is in B1 and the units are in B2, the formula would be =B1*B2.

User: Great, thank you. Quick unrelated question before I continue. What does the word "ephemeral" mean?

Assistant: "Ephemeral" describes something that lasts for a very short time. A soap bubble, a morning mist, or a social media story that disappears after a day are all ephemeral. It comes from a Greek word meaning "lasting only a day."

User: Nice. Now back to the shop. My cost to make each mug is 14.00 dollars. Given my selling price of 24.50, what is my markup percentage?

Assistant: Markup percentage is the profit per unit divided by the cost per unit. The profit per mug is 24.50 − 14.00 = 10.50. Then 10.50 ÷ 14.00 = 0.75, so your markup is 75 percent. In a spreadsheet, you could use =(B1-B3)/B3, formatted as a percentage, where B3 holds the cost.

User: Good. Is markup the same thing as profit margin?

Assistant: No, they are different. Markup is measured against cost, while margin is measured against the selling price. For your mug, the markup is 10.50 ÷ 14.00 = 75 percent. The margin would be 10.50 ÷ 24.50, which is about 42.86 percent. The same dollar profit gives two different percentages because the denominators differ.
